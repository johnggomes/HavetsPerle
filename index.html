A busy fish counter in Sønderborg.

Havets Perle opened in June 2025. It sells fresh fish, tapas and catering, and already supplies local restaurants and hotels. It moves about tonnes of fillet a week.

All of that fish lands in the north, at Skagen, Hanstholm and Thyborøn. It reaches the shop through processors who cut it, mark it up and set the timetable. The shop is the only part of this business that exists today.

Our own knife and cold store at Padborg.

We buy whole fish at auction, cut it ourselves at Padborg and send it out from the one place Denmark's fish trucks already pass on the way south. Als first, then restaurants across Denmark and into Europe.

Why Padborg

Padborg is the last logistics stop on Danish soil before the German border. Blue Water, H. P. Therkelsen, Thermo-Transit and Constellation Cold all run fish and cold-chain operations there. In 2024, exporters who had not booked early found trucks and terminals full by the second half of the year. The capacity is real and it is tight.

Sources: Logistics Business on Thermo-Transit · H. P. Therkelsen · Blue Water Padborg · Constellation Cold
The man

Korosh Abdi fished the North Sea before he sold fish on Perlegade.

Who he is
  • Dedicated and reliable. He is building a business to last, not to flip.
  • He genuinely loves fish, and he loves people.
  • He serves the chef and the walk-in customer with the same care. That is how you win a town.
What he has done
  • Opened Havets Perle in June 2025 and has run ahead of his own plan since.
  • Took ground fast in Sønderborg. The competing fishmonger on the street closed in January 2026.
  • Turned away orders for lack of space (local press, February 2026) and rents a freezer trailer to hold stock.
  • Investors have approached him unprompted.
Why him, why now

He can buy at the source, he knows exactly where the margin and the time are lost, and he's the one who spotted that Padborg fixes both.

Access to the catch

His years on trawlers give him direct contacts with skippers. He already buys crab, lobster and shrimp straight from auction. That access is the hardest thing to copy.

The barrier others can't cross

About 95% of businesses his size buy pre-cut fillets, because filleting at scale is hard. With the facility, he will step through that gap.

The idea is his

Why now

The shop has outgrown its space, demand is there, the street competitor has gone and capital is interested. The window is open.

What the money builds

Working capital is the cash to buy fish at auction while we wait days for customers to pay. It is the biggest single line and the least certain.

Beyond the cheque

The capital is what we need. From the right partner, these come as a bonus:
